# Render (layout, widgets, and the frame)

## Context and Design Philosophy

This leaf owns everything between a `GameState` snapshot and the cells on screen: how the frame divides into regions, what each region draws, how the layout responds to size, how numbers and names are formatted to fit, and what a frame is allowed to cost.

Its guiding property is that **a frame is a pure function of (snapshot, view state, terminal size)**. Nothing is cached between frames, no game value is mirrored shell-side, and no drawing decision depends on what was drawn last. This mirrors the determinism the sim core is built on, and it buys the same thing: layout becomes testable without a terminal. A frame rendered to an in-memory buffer at an exact size can be asserted against, so "the inspector appears at 100 columns" and "nothing overflows at 80×24" are tests rather than things someone remembers to check. Given how much of the navigation leaf's intent is stated as size-dependent behaviour, that testability is the difference between those specs being verified and being hoped for.

The leaf inherits its vocabulary rather than inventing it: glyphs and the meaning of every marker come from `navigation`, colour roles from `theme`, and the time available per frame from `runtime`. What is decided here is *arrangement*.

## The Frame

Vertically the frame is fixed furniture around a flexible body:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ header                                     1 │
│ tab bar                                    1 │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                              │
│ body                              height − 4 │
│                                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ticker                                     T │
│ hint bar                                   1 │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Body height is `height − 3 − T`, where `T` is the ticker's rows — one at the floor, and more only where width allows the navigation leaf's wider log. A formula rather than a table of sizes, so no arithmetic drifts as the chrome changes. At the 80×24 floor `T` is 1 and the body is 20 rows. When the offline banner is present it takes one row from the body rather than adding to the frame, so the body absorbs every variation and the frame always sums to the terminal's height.

The **header rule** is drawn in place rather than given a row, so it never enters the formula. It is the header row's bottom edge: an underline across every cell of the header row, whatever the header draws in them — facts, chips, the blank columns between, the padding — at every width step the header takes (TUI-NAV-086). An underline is a mark beneath a cell's character rather than a character, so the rule changes no glyph or word on the row, and each keeps the colour it would wear without the rule. A terminal that draws no underlines shows no rule, and nothing is substituted for it: the rule carries no information the header's words do not. Its colour belongs to the theme leaf.

Horizontally the body divides by width:

| Width | Body |
|---|---|
| under 80 columns *or* 24 rows | The too-small card; nothing else is drawn, and the sim keeps ticking |
| 80 – 99 | sidebar │ list — the inspector swaps in over the list on demand |
| 100 – 119 | sidebar │ list │ inspector, the inspector taking what remains |
| ≥ 120 | sidebar │ list (capped) │ inspector, extra width going to the inspector |

The sidebar is a fixed width; the list takes what it needs up to a cap; the inspector absorbs the remainder. Capping the list is deliberate — a row stretched across 150 columns puts its name and its cost so far apart that reading one line becomes a saccade, and the width is better spent on the inspector, which has genuinely more to say.

Side-by-side panes are separated by a **pane divider**: one column of `│`, the single-width light vertical box-drawing glyph, running the full height of the panes it separates. Each divider is taken from the list's space, on the edge that faces the neighbouring pane — the sidebar's side at every width, and the inspector's side as well where the inspector is a pane of its own — so 80 – 99 columns draw one divider and 100 columns and wider draw two. Whatever occupies the list's space fits its lines to the width the dividers leave, and the layout's widths are otherwise unchanged. The list gives the column because the sidebar's fixed width and the inspector's minimum are sized to what those panes hold, while the list's rows already shorten with an ellipsis the inspector resolves (Numbers and Names).

The divider belongs to the layout on screen. While the narrow-mode swap puts the inspector in over the list, the divider stays, taken from the same space; within a breakpoint's dead-band it follows whichever layout is drawn; and where a full-screen overlay takes the body there are no side-by-side panes and so no divider, and nothing cuts through the overlay's text. It spans the panes' rows only — never the banner row above them, the ticker, or the hint bar. Its cells hold the glyph and nothing else: a focused row's reverse video, an armed row's expansion, and the list's position indicator all end at the width the dividers leave. Its colour belongs to the theme leaf.

## Breakpoints

Two breakpoints, at 100 and 120 columns. The first is where the inspector stops being a swap and becomes a pane; the second is where the list stops growing and hands surplus width to the inspector.

Both carry a dead-band, so a terminal sitting exactly on a boundary does not flip layouts as the pane wobbles by a column (TUI-NAV-047). The dead-band is asymmetric: growing into a layout happens at the boundary, and falling out of it happens a few columns below, so a resize that overshoots and settles does not thrash.

## The List Viewport

Lists outgrow the body — 59 ideas will not fit 20 rows — so the list scrolls, and the scroll position is derived rather than stored: it is whatever keeps the cursor visible with a small margin above and below. Deriving it preserves the pure-function property and means the viewport can never disagree with the cursor after a reveal inserts rows or a resize changes the body height.

A group header scrolls like any other line. Pinning the current group to the top was considered and dropped: it costs a row of the body permanently, and `[` / `]` already move by group, which is the affordance a player reaches for when they want to know where they are.

**A group header's line.** A group header (navigation leaf: Groups within a section) is one line of the list, drawn as a labelled rule across the list's whole width: from the list's left edge, two `─`, a space, the header's words, a space, and `─` repeated to the list's right edge — `── kittens ─────────────`. It takes the cursor's column like the rest of the line and carries no cursor glyph, marker, count, or cost, so it never reads as a row: a row begins with its cursor column and its marker, a header with the rule. Every cell of it wears `dim`, the words included, whatever the rows beneath it wear (theme leaf: `dim` carries a list's group-header rules), so it says where a kind begins and nothing about any row's state. The words are drawn whole wherever the list holds the leading rule, the words, and a space; a list narrower than that cuts the words as a name is cut (TUI-RENDER-014).

When the list is longer than the viewport, the frame says so — a position indicator rather than a scrollbar, since a single column of scrollbar is both harder to read and more expensive in a narrow pane than a short "n more below".

## The Pointer Record

A pointer event is resolved against the frame the player was looking at (navigation leaf: The Pointer), and only the drawing knows where that frame put each thing. So every frame, as it draws, yields a **pointer record**: the terminal size it was drawn at, and the cells of each thing on it that a pointer can address, each naming what it is.

- *Each tab.* The cells of the tab's label as the tab bar drew it — its `★` badge where it wears one, its digit, and its name where the bar draws the name, in the full form or the compact strip (TUI-NAV-034) — naming the tab's section. The blank columns between labels belong to no tab.
- *Each row in view.* For each row of the focused section's list that the frame drew within the list's viewport (TUI-RENDER-009), the cells of the row's line within the list's space — from the list's left edge to the width the dividers leave (TUI-RENDER-016) — naming the row by its identity, as the cursor remembers it (TUI-NAV-008), and its section. A row scrolled out of the viewport, or folded in the done group, was not drawn and has no cells. A group header, the done header, an overflow note, an armed row's summary lines, and the blank lines below the last row are lines of the list but not rows, and name none.
- *The list.* The cells of the list's space, naming the focused section's list, so the wheel can be over the list anywhere in it — on a row, a header, a note, or the blank lines below.
- *The Journal.* While the Journal is drawn, the cells of its region, with how far it can scroll: the scroll at which its last line stands at the foot of the region, or none where every line fits.
- *The sidebar.* While the sidebar is drawn, the cells of its region, with the stores it drew from in the order it listed them, the place in that order of the first store it drew, how many stores it drew, and the furthest place its first store can stand — where the last store stands at its foot — or none where every store fits. The scroll keys and the wheel move the sidebar from where this frame drew it, as the Journal's do (navigation leaf: Scrolling the sidebar).

The record names only what the frame drew. Where the narrow-mode swap, the palette, the Journal, or help holds the list's space, it names no row and no list; where a full-screen overlay holds the body, it names no sidebar, the overlay drawing in its place; and where a full-screen overlay holds the body, the tab bar beside it is still drawn and still recorded, its tabs' meaning under the overlay being the navigation leaf's to say. A frame that draws the too-small card (TUI-RENDER-004) records no target at all, and nor does the waking screen (navigation leaf: The Waking Screen), which is drawn in the frame's place.

The record is a second output of the pure function, never an input to one. Drawing consults no record of an earlier frame — the loop keeps the last one only to hand to navigation with the pointer events that arrive before the next frame — so a frame is still a function of its snapshot, its view state, and its size alone (TUI-RENDER-001), and recording costs only what drawing already visits (TUI-RENDER-015).

## Numbers and Names

Space is the scarce resource here, and the tenet ordering decides how to spend it: whimsy names the row, but the plain truth must be reachable (tenet 3).

**Numbers are compact where they are glanced at and exact where they are read.** The sidebar and list rows abbreviate to a fixed width, so columns stay aligned and a value's magnitude is legible at a glance. The inspector states exact values, unabbreviated — TUI-NAV-018 requires exact per-component costs there, and that is the contract: every figure the sidebar or a list row abbreviates has its exact form in the inspector, a row's figures on that row and the sidebar's on its store's row in the stores group (TUI-NAV-049).

The forms below are stated for the figures of the regions this leaf lays out field by field — the sidebar, the list's rows, and the inspector — and of the two things drawn for a row within them, the count prompt and the armed row's summary. A figure set inside a line of words elsewhere — the header, the ticker, the report banner, the Journal, a footer note, the hint bar — stands in a sentence the navigation leaf composes. The navigation leaf keeps the header's figures out of the compact form, since they belong to no row the inspector describes; which form the header's figures do take, and which the figures of the other lines of words take, neither leaf yet states (Open Questions, With navigation). The contract does not reach those figures: a report or a refusal has no row whose inspector could hold an exact form.

Precision follows magnitude rather than being fixed: a stock of 3 reads as `3`, not `3.00`. Trailing noise makes a number look more precise than it is and costs columns that alignment needs.

**The display floor.** A figure whose magnitude is below one billionth reads as zero in both forms below: `0`, with no sign, no `<0.001`, and no fraction. A value accumulated tick by tick carries binary residue — a store spent to empty can be left holding a sliver such as 0.00000000000000003, and a rate whose gains and drains cancel can come out a hair either side of zero. Drawn as it stands, residue reads as a trickle in the sidebar, as a long fraction of noise in the inspector, and as a sign on a store that is still. The floor sits where the core already stops telling a figure from nothing — its spends count a stock within a billionth of a price as meeting it (SIM-ECO-022) — and far below every figure the game defines, so it erases residue and nothing the player could act on.

**The compact form.** At most four digits, with a magnitude marker — `K`, `M`, `B`, `T`, `Q`, one for each three decades — once the rounded value reaches a thousand: `3`, `12.5`, `999.9`, `1.235K`, `12.35K`, `123.5M`. Rounding is to the nearest, a tie rounding away from zero, and a rounding that reaches a thousand of one marker carries into the next, so 999,960 reads `1M`, never `1000K`: every value has one compact spelling, whichever side of a boundary it came from. Below one, the leading `0` spends one of the four digits, so a fraction shows at most three decimal places: `0.5`, `0.125`, `0.004`. The form's range is bounded at both ends and says so rather than misreading at either: a value of at least the display floor that rounds to nothing in three places reads `<0.001`, so a trickle never reads as nothing; and a value that reaches a thousand `Q` once rounded — 999.95`Q` or more, which would otherwise carry past the last marker — or that is not finite, reads `>999Q`, a figure past anything the markers name. The form states a size — a stock, a cap, a count, a price — and a size is never less than nothing, so zero of either sign, a value below the display floor, and any figure below zero read `0`; the one signed figure these regions abbreviate is a rate, which carries its sign in the rate form, and the inspector's exact form states a figure below zero as it is. So a compact figure is never wider than six columns — `1.235K`, `<0.001`, and `>999Q` are the widest — and six columns is the field a compact figure is given.

**The rate form.** A store's net rate is a signed figure, the one these regions state beside the flows it is made of — which a stores row in the inspector lists beneath it, each drawn as the net rate is (navigation leaf: What moves a store) — and in both forms it is drawn behind its sign: `+` where the rate is above zero and the store gains, `-` where it is below zero and the store drains, and no sign where its size reads `0` — zero of either sign, or below the display floor. The sign is how a gain and a drain are told apart at a glance, so a gain is never left unsigned, in the sidebar or in the inspector; a still store is given none, since `+0` reads as a gain that is not happening and `-0` as a drain. In the sidebar the rate is the compact form of its size behind its sign — `+1.5`, `-12.35K`, `+<0.001`, `0` — seven columns at most. On a stores row in the inspector (TUI-NAV-080) it is the exact form of its size behind its sign — `+1.5`, `-12345.5`, `0`. Every other per-second figure the inspector states, such as a definition's per-tick field stated per game second (TUI-NAV-018), is a size rather than a store's gain or drain, and takes the exact form with no `+`.

**The exact form.** Every digit of the whole part, with no magnitude marker, no exponent, and no digit grouping, and a fraction rounded to the nearest, a tie rounding away from zero, at three decimal places or at the value's fourth significant figure, whichever lies further, trailing zeros dropped: `3`, `1234.5`, `1234567`, `0.25`, `0.1234`, `0.001235`. A figure below zero is written behind `-`; zero of either sign, and a figure below the display floor, reads `0`. Past the floor, a stock accumulated tick by tick still carries binary noise past any digit the game means — a value that should be 0.3 is stored as 0.30000000000000004 — and printing that noise claims a precision the value does not carry (TUI-RENDER-013). Three places keep every digit the compact form can show, and the fourth significant figure keeps a small value's digits down to the floor, so whatever the compact form rounds away the exact form still carries. The same holds at the other end: a float holds about sixteen significant digits, so a whole part longer than that is written with the fewest digits that read back as the same float and zeros in the places past them, never with the noise digits of the float's binary expansion.

A figure that is not finite has no digits to state, and is said in words instead. The core reports a store it does not cap as having a cap that is not finite (SIM-ECO-001), so on a stores row such a cap reads as the store having none (TUI-NAV-080). Every other figure that is not finite, in the inspector, a preview, or a summary, reads in plain words as beyond reach, the wording the armed summary uses for one (TUI-NAV-075).

**Figures the row's description does not state.** Two things drawn for a row carry figures that the inspector's description of the row (TUI-NAV-018) does not state: an open count prompt's preview (TUI-NAV-067), the price of a count the player is typing, and an armed row's consequence summary (TUI-NAV-075), the facts a heavy action is confirmed against. Both are read rather than glanced at, and abbreviating either would leave a figure whose exact form is nowhere to be found, the one thing the contract rules out. So both take the exact form wherever they are drawn: in place of the row's cost field, in the lines beneath an armed row, or in the narrow swap's inspector (TUI-NAV-035).

**Columns align by their right edge.** The figures that stand in a column — a store's stock and its rate in the sidebar, and a list row's count and the first figure of its cost — are each right-aligned in a field as wide as its form can be, so a column's last digits land on one column on every line whatever the values' magnitudes, and the eye compares sizes down the column's edge. The decimal points do not line up, and are not made to: precision follows magnitude and a marker stands where digits would, so there is no shared point to align on. Two kinds of figure stand in no column. A store's cap is the second half of a pair read together, and stands against the `/` before it (the sidebar, below). A figure that follows another within a field — a cost's later components, and every figure of a count prompt's text — runs on after what precedes it at its own width: how much precedes it differs from row to row, so no column could hold it.

- *The sidebar.* Each store is two lines. The first is its name at the left edge and, at the right edge, its stock and its cap as a pair: the stock right-aligned in six columns, a `/`, and the cap left-aligned in six columns, so every store's `/` stands at one column with its stock ending against it and its cap starting after it. A store the core reports uncapped has no cap to state (above), so its `/` and its cap's field are left blank: `>999Q` there would read as a cap that is merely enormous. A cap of zero is a cap, and reads `/0`. The name is spelled as the store's row in the stores group spells it, and takes the width the pair leaves, with at least one blank column before the pair, shortening with an ellipsis where it must (TUI-RENDER-014), its full spelling on that stores row. The cursor is never on a sidebar line, so a name cut there into a spelling another store's name shares could be told apart only by leaving for the stores group; the name column is therefore never narrower than the narrowest width at which every two names in the resource vocabulary that differ whole still differ shortened to it. That width is fixed by the vocabulary rather than by which stores are revealed, so the sidebar is at least that width, the blank column, and the pair's thirteen columns wide, and no reveal can make two of its names read alike. The second line is its fill bar at the left edge, its rate right-aligned in a seven-column field after the bar, and, while the store is capped, the `full` tag after the rate (TUI-NAV-022), a space between each. So every store's stock ends at one column, every cap starts at one column, every bar starts at one column, every rate ends at one column, and every tag starts at one column. The bar line is at most seventeen columns — four of bar, seven of rate, four of tag, and the two spaces — so the tag, the capped store's meaning without colour, stays whole at every figure the store can show.
  - *Which stores it draws.* The sidebar draws the stores the stores group lists (TUI-NAV-049), each whole — both its lines — or not at all, so no store is cut between its figures and its `full`, and the stores it draws stand in the order the stores group lists them. Where its lines hold every listed store, it draws them all and has nothing to scroll. Where they do not, it draws a window onto them: consecutive stores in that order, from the first store the player has scrolled it to (navigation leaf: Scrolling the sidebar). Where listed stores stand above the window, the sidebar's first line is a note, `▴ N more`; where listed stores stand below it, its last line is a note, `▾ N more`; N is the count of listed stores on that side, written in full. Between its notes the window holds as many whole stores as those lines hold at two lines each, and it never runs past the last store: where the first store scrolled to would leave the sidebar's foot empty below the last store, the window begins as far down the list as still fills the sidebar, the last store standing at its foot. So what the sidebar draws changes only when the player scrolls it, a store joins the list, or the pane is resized; a store that fills or spends keeps its place, and nothing in the sidebar moves on its own. Every store a note counts stands whole, with its exact figures, on its row in the stores group.
  - *A note's full count.* Where any of the stores a note counts wears `full`, the note goes on with ` · M full`, M the count of those that do — `▾ 6 more · 2 full` — so a capped store out of the window still says it wants notice; where none does, the note is its triangle, count, and `more` alone. The note is drawn in `dim`, save its `M full`, which wears `attention`, as the `full` tag on a store's line does (TUI-THEME-015). A note is drawn whole; where the sidebar is narrower than a whole note, its ` more` gives way, the triangle, the count, and the full count standing — `▾ 6 · 2 full`.
- *List rows.* A row's owned count and next cost stand in fields of their own at the row's right, after the name (TUI-NAV-009), with one blank column between the name's column and the first field and one between the two fields. Each field draws what the inspector's description of the row states under that heading (TUI-NAV-018), so the row and its description never disagree, and a row whose description states nothing there leaves the field blank — save a kitten's row, whose two fields' place carries the kitten's state (below).
  - *The count field* draws the owned count the inspector states for the row as `×` directly before a compact figure, the two right-aligned together in seven columns, so the glyph stays joined to the figure it counts, as the row's anatomy draws it, and every count ends at one column. A count of zero leaves the field blank: `×0` down a list of rows not yet built is noise, and in a column of counts a blank reads as none.
  - *The cost field* draws the price the inspector states as the row's next cost (TUI-NAV-078, TUI-NAV-073): its components, in the order the core names them, each a compact figure, a space, and the resource's name spelled as its row in the stores group spells it, so a resource reads one way in the sidebar, the list, and the inspector; the components stand apart by `, `. The need, have, marks, and waits the inspector sets beside each component are the inspector's alone. The first figure is right-aligned in the field's first six columns, so every row's first cost figure ends at one column. The field's width is fixed: six columns of figure, a space, the widest name any resource in the vocabulary is spelled with on its stores row, and three columns more, the width of `, …`. That width belongs to the vocabulary, not to the state, so it is the same in every frame and no frame measures it (TUI-RENDER-015). A cost is drawn as the longest leading run of its components that fits the field with, where any component is left out, `, …` after the last one drawn: the separator every component stands behind, then an ellipsis for the components the field could not hold. So the ellipsis reads as one more item in the list, never as a name cut short (TUI-RENDER-014), and since the field holds the widest single component with `, …` after it, the first component always draws whole. No figure is drawn in part, since a figure drawn in part reads as a wrong number. Every component, the ones left out here included, is stated in the inspector.
  - *A kitten's row.* A kitten's row states neither an owned count nor a next cost (TUI-NAV-018), so in the Clowder list, which gives both fields, its fields' place — the count field, the blank column between, and the cost field — would stand blank. That place carries instead the word for the kitten's state that the navigation leaf names (Action Model: what a kitten's row says it is doing): begun at the count field's left edge, one blank column after the name's column, and left-aligned, so every kitten's word starts at one column down the roster; drawn in the row's own style, as the row's fields are; running into the blank column and the cost field's place as far as it needs; and blank after it to the field's end. It is the one row on which the fields' place draws something the inspector does not state under their headings, and it is a word rather than a figure, so the contract that every abbreviated figure has its exact form in the inspector does not reach it. The place is the count field's seven columns, a blank, and the cost field's width, fixed by the resource vocabulary as that width is, and it is wider than every name in the job vocabulary and either word, so the word is drawn whole at every width the list takes and never cut. The name's column takes the width the fields leave, as on every row, so the word moves no name and changes nothing about where an `(n: Elder)` hint fits (TUI-NAV-095). A kitten's command takes no count, so no count prompt opens on its row and TUI-RENDER-021 never moves its word.
  - *The tinkerers' bench row.* The bench states neither an owned count nor a next cost either, and its fields' place carries its seats instead, in the words the navigation leaf gives them (The tinkerers' bench): drawn as a kitten's word is — begun at the count field's left edge and left-aligned, in the row's own style, blank after it to the cost field's end — so the bench's summary starts at the column every count starts at. Its figures are seat counts, small whole numbers, so the whole text fits the place at every width with figures of up to two digits. Where it would not, the ` · N waiting` clause gives way whole first, since the inspector states every waiting kitten by name; and where the rest still would not, it is cut as a name is cut (TUI-RENDER-014), the inspector stating its seats line whole. The bench takes no command, so no count prompt opens on its row.
  - *Which lists give the fields width.* A list gives a field width only where its section can list a row whose description states that heading — an owned count, or a next cost — judged by the section alone, never by which rows are listed or what they hold now, so no field comes or goes as a row is revealed, a figure changes, or the list scrolls, and deciding it costs a frame nothing (TUI-RENDER-015). The name column takes the width the fields leave, so on every row the names start at one column and are cut at one width.
- *The count prompt's row.* While a count prompt is open on a listed row (TUI-NAV-011), that row draws the prompt's text in its cost field's place, or, in a list that gives no cost field, in the place one would take: the count, then the preview (TUI-NAV-067).
  - *The count* is `×` directly before the digits the player has typed, exactly as typed, leading zeros kept, and `×` alone while none are typed. It is the field's echo, so every digit pressed shows where it was typed, and the count on screen is the count `Enter` will use (the prompt's bound keeps it short). The count is never cut.
  - *The preview* follows the count where it prices components: two blank columns, then the components drawn as the cost field's are — a figure, a space, and the resource's stores-row name, apart by `, ` — each figure in the exact form. Where the preview is the count alone, or nothing, nothing follows the count. The two blank columns keep the count's digits from reading into the first figure's.
  - *Its width.* The text begins at the left edge of the cost field's place and runs rightward. Where it is longer than that place, the row's count field, where the list gives one, and its name move left by what it needs, the count field keeping its figure and its seven columns, and the name shortening as far as a single character and its ellipsis. Text that still does not fit keeps the count whole and draws the longest leading run of components that fits with `, …` after it where any is left out, as the cost field does; where not even the first component fits, the count is followed by its two blank columns and `…`. Only the prompt's row gives way, and only while the prompt is open; every other row keeps its columns.
  - *In the narrow swap.* Where the inspector holds the list's space and carries the prompt (TUI-NAV-035), the prompt's count and preview take the same text.

**A resource's name in a field wears its coat.** Wherever this leaf draws a resource's name as a field — in the sidebar, on its stores row, in a cost field, in a count prompt's preview, and at the head of the inspector's cost lines — the name's characters wear the colours the theme leaf gives that resource (theme leaf: Coats), on the lines that leaf says may carry one, and only the name wears them: the figures, separators, and blank columns beside it wear the line's own colour, so a column of figures still reads as one column. A shortened name's ellipsis wears the colour of the character it replaces, so a store's leading characters look the same at every width.

**Names truncate, but never into ambiguity.** Where a name will not fit, it is shortened with an explicit ellipsis so the player can see that something was cut. A truncated name is always resolvable. In the list, the inspector shows it in full, and the cursor is already on it. In the sidebar, where no cursor stands, the floor under the name column keeps every cut name distinct from every other in place, and the store's row in the stores group spells it whole.

## The Portrait

A kitten's `about` card in the inspector opens with its portrait (navigation leaf: Who the kitten is): the kitten drawn small, in single-width characters only, in the pose its profile holds (kittens leaf: Poses) — sitting up, napping, or lying in a loaf. Each pose is a fixed drawing.

Sitting up, five rows:

```
   /\_/\
  ( o.o )
   > ^ <
  /█████\  __
 (_█████_)██/
```

Napping, four rows:

```
         /\_/\
   _____( -.- )
  /█████████  \
 (_███████████_)
```

Loaf, four rows:

```
      /\_/\
  ___( o.o )
 /██████████\
(_██████████_)
```

Rows and columns are numbered from zero, from each drawing's top-left cell. Each cell has one of three roles:

- **Fur** — the `█` cells, painted in the fur's colours (theme leaf: Fur).
- **Eye** — the two `o` cells of a pose whose kitten is awake, the left eye first: sitting up, row 1 columns 4 and 6; the loaf, row 1 columns 7 and 9. The napping kitten's eyes are closed, so its pose has no eye cells, and its eyes are told in words alone.
- **Outline** — every other non-blank cell, the nose and the whiskers included.

The outline is drawn in the inherited foreground, so the shape of the cat stands against any ground whatever its fur. Blank cells stay blank: the portrait paints nothing around the cat. The head is outline and the body is fur, so a cat's face is always the familiar one and its colouring shows on the body, where a pattern has room to read.

**Where each fur cell is on the cat.** A pattern lays its colours by where a cell sits on the body, not by its position in one particular drawing, so the theme leaf states each pattern's mask once for every pose. Each fur cell is one of six parts — **top** (the shoulders or the back nearest the head), **back**, **chest**, **under** (the belly and lower body), **paw**, **tail** — and some cells also belong to **patch A** or **patch B**, the two sets a patched coat's second and third colours fall on:

**Drawn whole.** Each of the portrait's rows is drawn whole from the inspector's left edge, never wrapped, shortened, or trimmed of its leading blanks, since a row moved by a column is a different and broken picture. The inspector is never narrower than its minimum, which is wider than every pose.

**Its words.** The fur's words and the eyes' words stand beside the cat, three blank columns to the right of the pose's widest row, the fur's on row 1 and the eyes' on row 2 — wherever the inspector's width holds the pose, the three columns, and the longer of the two whole. Where it does not, they stand beneath the portrait instead, the fur's line and then the eyes', and wrap as any inspector line does. Which way they stand follows from the width, the pose, and the two lengths alone.

**The strip.** Beneath the portrait, and beneath its words where they stand beneath it, the swatch strip (theme leaf: a swatch) lists the fur's colours, each swatch with its colour's name, items two blank columns apart. It breaks onto a further line only between items, never parting a swatch from its name, each further line beginning at the inspector's left edge.

## The Frame Budget

The loop gives render a slice of each frame after input and ticks have taken theirs, and rendering must fit in it. Nothing here should come close: a terminal frame is a few thousand cells, and the expensive-looking parts (46 resources, long lists) are bounded by the viewport, which never exceeds the body's rows.

The two things that could get expensive are worth naming so they are not discovered later. Deriving the sidebar's rates asks the core for a value per revealed resource every frame, and the palette's per-cell work is proportional to what is drawn rather than what exists. Both are bounded, neither is obviously a problem, and both are cheap to measure once there is something to measure — which is the honest state of every frame-cost question at this point.

Whether frames run at a fixed cadence or only when something changed is deferred jointly with `runtime`, whose loop owns the timing. The pure-function property makes the dirty-frame optimisation available later without restructuring: if a frame is a function of its inputs, then "did the inputs change" is a well-formed question.

## Decisions & Alternatives

| Decision | Chosen | Alternatives Considered | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame derivation | Pure function of snapshot, view state, and size | Incremental/dirty-region drawing; cached widget state | Testable at exact sizes without a terminal, which is what makes the size-dependent navigation specs verifiable; leaves the optimisation available later |
| Chrome height | Body is `height − 4`; chrome is constant | Per-breakpoint row tables | A formula cannot drift out of sync as chrome changes; the banner takes from the body rather than growing the frame |
| Header rule | An underline across the header row's cells, drawn in place | A rule row of its own beneath the header | A row of its own costs the body a row at the 80×24 floor, where the body is already 20 rows; the underline marks the same edge for no space and leaves the frame's algebra untouched |
| Pane divider | One `│` column at each boundary between side-by-side panes, taken from the list's space | Taking the column from the sidebar or the inspector; a blank gutter column | The sidebar's fixed width and the inspector's minimum are sized to their contents, while the list's rows already shorten legibly; a blank column separates the panes less plainly and leaves the chrome's accent nothing to sit on |
| Wide layout | Cap the list, give surplus to the inspector | Let the list grow; add a fourth column | A row stretched across 150 columns separates name from cost by a saccade; the inspector has more to say |
| Scroll position | Derived from the cursor each frame | Stored scroll offset | Stored offset can disagree with the cursor after a reveal or a resize; deriving it cannot |
| Group headers | Scroll with the list | Pin the current group | Pinning costs a permanent body row for what `[` / `]` already provides |
| A group header's line | A `dim` rule spanning the list with the header's words set in it near its left end | The words alone between short dashes; the words in bold or a palette colour; a blank line above | A rule that spans the list marks the break across every column the rows use, so the eye sees where a kind ends without reading the words; a colour or weight would say something about state that a header does not carry; a blank line costs a body row per group |
| Overflow indicator | A short "more below" note | A scrollbar column | A column is expensive in a narrow pane and harder to read at a glance |
| Where pointer targets come from | A record each frame yields as it draws, naming rows by identity | Navigation working the layout and the list's window out again for each event; a record naming rows by their position in the list | Only the drawing knows where the frame put each thing: a layout worked out again can disagree with the one on screen, its dead-band following the frame before, and a key earlier in the same batch can have scrolled the list. A position goes stale when an earlier event moves rows, where an identity still names the row that was drawn under the click |
| Number precision | Compact when glanced, exact in the inspector; precision follows magnitude | Fixed decimal places everywhere; exact everywhere | Fixed places make small values look falsely precise and eat the columns alignment needs; exact everywhere does not fit |
| Reach of the contract | The sidebar and list rows, each abbreviation with its exact form in the inspector | Every figure on screen held to having an exact form in the inspector | A report or a refusal has no row whose inspector could hold an exact form, so a figure in a banner, the Journal, a footer, or the hint bar cannot be held to one |
| Compact form's reach | Four digits and a marker per three decades, `K` to `Q`, bounded by `<0.001` below and `>999Q` above | Scientific notation; markers without end; a fraction showing every leading zero | Scientific notation is notation to learn on a screen meant to be read at a glance; an unbounded form cannot be given a fixed field; a fraction's leading zeros grow it past the field. Past either bound the glance needs only that the figure is tiny or enormous, and the exact digits are in the inspector |
| Display floor | A figure below one billionth reads as zero in both forms, with no sign | No floor; the compact form's smallest figure as the floor, so a value that rounds to nothing in three places reads `0` | With no floor, residue left by a spend or by a rate whose gains and drains cancel reads as a trickle in the sidebar, a long fraction in the inspector, and a sign on a still store. The game defines figures that round to nothing in three places — a slow store's gain, a definition's per-second field — so a floor there would draw a gaining store as still and state a field as the nothing it departs from (TUI-NAV-018). A billionth is where the core already stops telling a figure from nothing (SIM-ECO-022), far below any figure the game defines |
| Compact rounding | To the nearest, carrying into the next marker | Truncating toward zero, so a stock never reads as reaching a price it has not; keeping the marker a value started under (`1000K`) | Whether a price is met is the row's marker's to say (TUI-NAV-009), not the digits'; truncation biases every stock and rate low to guard a comparison the marker already makes. A value with two spellings either side of a boundary reads as two magnitudes |
| Exact form's fraction | Three places, or to the fourth significant figure where that is further; no digit grouping; a long whole part written with the digits that identify the float and zeros after them | The float's full expansion; a fixed number of significant figures; grouping separators | The full expansion prints binary noise as though it were precision, in the fraction of a small value and the whole part of a huge one alike; a significant-figure limit drops whole-number digits from a large stock, which is no longer exact; a comma between digit groups reads as the comma between a price's components |
| A figure that is not finite, read | In words: a store's uncapped cap as having none, anything else as beyond reach | `>999Q`, as the compact form has it; the `∞` glyph | `>999Q` says a figure is enormous where the core means there is no limit at all; `∞` is outside the single-width glyph set the navigation leaf allows |
| Preview and armed summary figures | Exact form wherever drawn | Compact, as the rest of the row is | The inspector's description of the row does not state their figures, so abbreviating them leaves an abbreviation with no exact form to find |
| Column alignment | Each figure that stands in a column right-aligned in a field as wide as its form can be; a cost's later components and a preview's figures run on at their own width | Left-aligned figures; aligning on the decimal point; fields sized to the widest figure in view; a field for every component of a cost | Left alignment puts magnitudes out of register; precision follows magnitude, so there is no shared decimal point; a field sized to what is in view moves every column when a figure grows a digit or the list scrolls. Rows' costs have different numbers of components, so a field for each would leave most rows' later fields blank and spend the width of the widest price on every row |
| A store's net rate | Signed in both forms: `+` above zero, `-` below, none otherwise | Signed in the sidebar only, the inspector's exact form writing a gain unsigned | The sign is how a gain is told from a drain, and a gain read unsigned in the inspector beside a signed one in the sidebar reads as two different facts about one store |
| Sidebar lines | Name, stock, and cap; then bar, rate, and `full` | Name alone, then stock, cap, bar, rate, and `full`; the cap left off the sidebar | Every figure and the tag on one line run past a sidebar narrow enough to be a glance once the figures reach their widest, and the cut falls on the tag, the capped store's one non-colour channel; beside the name, the figures cost only name width, which an ellipsis gives up legibly so long as no two names are cut into one spelling (Sidebar name floor). The cap is what the bar fills toward, and without it the bar's scale cannot be read at a glance |
| Sidebar name floor | The name column never narrower than the narrowest width at which every two resource names that differ whole still differ shortened | Every name whole; a fixed number of columns for the name | The cursor is never on a sidebar line, so a cut name must still be told from every other in place. Keeping every name whole spends list width at every terminal size, which the list's fields need, to spare cuts that the floor already keeps unambiguous; a fixed count of columns has no reason in the names it must hold, and is too narrow for one vocabulary and wasteful for another |
| Stock and cap | A pair about a `/` at one column, the stock right-aligned before it and the cap left-aligned after it; nothing for an uncapped store | The cap right-aligned in a field of its own; `>999Q` or a word for an uncapped store | A right-aligned cap leaves blank columns between the `/` and the cap, so the pair reads as two stray figures; an uncapped store's blank pair is read by what it lacks, where `>999Q` reads as a large cap and a word costs the name columns on every store |
| List row fields | Count and cost in fixed fields at the row's right, each drawing what the inspector states for the row, the `×` joined to its figure, the cost field as wide as one component with the vocabulary's widest name and `, …` | Count and cost following the name at whatever width each takes; the `×` at the count field's left edge; a cost field sized to the costs of every row listed, or of the rows in view; the cost's first component alone | Fields that follow the name move with every name's length and every figure's width, so neither counts nor costs can be read down the list; a `×` held at the field's edge stands up to five columns from the figure it counts; sizing to every row listed prices rows no frame draws (TUI-RENDER-015), and sizing to the rows in view moves every column as the list scrolls. The first component alone hides a price's other needs with no mark that any were left out, where an ellipsis says so |
| A cost the field cannot hold | Its longest leading run of whole components, then `, …` | `…` joined to the last component's name; the field cut at its edge | Joined to a name, the ellipsis reads as that name cut short (TUI-RENDER-014); standing behind the separator every component stands behind, it reads as one more item, the ones not shown. A cut at the edge can fall mid-figure, which reads as a wrong number |
| A kitten's state on its row | In its count and cost fields' place, begun at the count field's left edge and left-aligned, whole | After the name in the name's column, drawn whole or not at all; a column of its own on every row | At the 80-column floor the name's column cannot hold a longer name, the `(n: Elder)` hint, and a longer job's name together, so a word drawn whole or not at all there would be missing from many rows at the width most play starts at, and cutting names to make room costs the row its identity. The fields' place stands blank on every kitten's row and its width is fixed by the vocabulary, so the word is whole at every width and moves nothing. A column of its own spends width on every row of every list for a fact only kitten rows have |
| A zero count on a list row | Blank | `×0` | A column of `×0` down the rows not yet built is noise the eye must read past to find the counts that matter; in a column of counts a blank already reads as none |
| The count prompt's text | In the cost field's place: `×` and the digits as typed, then two blank columns and the priced components; the count field keeps its figure | The typed count in the count field's place; the digits rewritten as the number they make, leading zeros dropped; the count after the components | The count field's column holds what is owned, down every row, so a typed count there reads as copies held, and the owned count is what a player weighs a count against. An echo that drops a digit the player pressed reads as a lost keypress. Leading, the count stays put as the components change with it |
| Sidebar overflow | A window onto the stores in the stores group's order, moved only by the player (navigation leaf: Scrolling the sidebar); every store in it whole; a note at the head and the foot counting the stores out of view on that side, and how many of them wear `full` | A fixed glance choosing the stores wearing `full` first, with one `+N more` note; clipping at the sidebar's foot; the stores wearing `full` drawn at the top | A fixed glance keeps every store past its lines out of the sidebar for good once a meadow holds more stores than it has lines, and its full-first choice moves stores in and out of the glance as they fill and spend. The window moves only when the player moves it, so a glance finds each store where it last stood. The notes' full count keeps what the full-first choice was for: a capped store out of view still says it wants notice. A clipped foot drops stores with no mark that any are missing, and can cut a store between its lines; stores wearing `full` drawn at the top would jump lines as they fill and spend |
| A resource's name in a price | Spelled as its stores row spells it | A lower-cased word of its own | Two spellings of one resource read as two resources, and the stores row is where the player reads that resource's stock, cap, and rate |
| Truncation | Explicit ellipsis, full text in the inspector | Hard cut; scrolling text; shrinking to fit | A hard cut hides that anything was lost; motion in a glanceable pane is exactly what the design is avoiding |
| The portrait's art | Three whole-cat poses — sitting up, napping, a loaf — each an outline head over a body of `█` fur cells | A face filled solid with fur; one pose for every kitten; braille or half-block pixel art | A face filled with fur and two small eyes reads as a mask rather than a cat. The familiar outline face keeps every cat friendly-looking on any ground, and the body gives a pattern room to show. Three poses make a roster look like a room of cats each getting on with its day. Braille and half-blocks need a painted ground to show colour on both halves of a cell, and the ground is the terminal's |
| Where the portrait's words go | Beside the cat where they fit whole, otherwise beneath | Always beneath; always beside, wrapping | Beside is the card the portrait was designed for, and it saves two rows; words wrapped beside a narrow picture break into fragments, so a pane too narrow takes them beneath instead |

## Open Questions & Future Decisions

### Deferred

Every question here is one that a real terminal answers better than a document, which is why the layout above is stated as an algebra rather than a set of measurements.

1. The sidebar's exact width and the list's cap. Both are named as roles above; their values want a real pane and real content lengths. The sidebar is at least as wide as its widest bar line, seventeen columns, and as its figure line with the narrowest name column that keeps every store's name distinct: that column, one blank column, and the pair's thirteen (Numbers and Names). Each column past that floor goes to the names.
2. The dead-band's width, and whether asymmetric hysteresis is enough to stop thrash during a drag-resize.
3. Frame cadence — fixed rate versus redraw-on-change — decided jointly with `runtime` once frame cost is measurable.
4. Whether the ticker earns more than one row when height allows. The navigation leaf permits up to four lines when wide; whether that is better than giving the rows to the body is a judgement about a real game's event volume.

### With navigation

1. The form of a figure set in a line of words the navigation leaf composes — the header, the ticker, the report banner, the Journal, a footer note, the hint bar. The navigation leaf rules the compact form out for the header's figures (its Decisions & Alternatives, the header at a narrow width) and states no form for any of them, and the forms under Numbers and Names are stated for this leaf's own regions. Whether the header's figures take the exact form or a form of their own, and which form the figures of the other lines take, is for the two leaves to settle together; until then no form is stated for them.
